# Monodromy group computation

A monodromy group is the image of π₁ of the base of a family acting on
the cohomology of the fiber. Computing it means identifying that image
as a subgroup of GL (or O/Sp) of a lattice. That is a different job from
computing intersection numbers.

Lean in this PR (`Hodge/PlaneSpanPairing.lean`) does the Gram numbers.
It does not compute Mon.

## The setup

Take a smooth family X → B of fourfolds (smooth sextics in P^5, or the
thinner locus of sextics that contain a fixed plane Π).

A loop in B based at b₀ drags cycles around the family and returns an
automorphism of H^4(X_b0, Z), or of (h²)^⊥ after primitive projection.

    Mon := im(ρ) ⊆ O(H^4_prim(X, Z), intersection form).

For a general smooth sextic, b₄ = 2606, so the primitive lattice has
rank 2605. A computation of Mon is a description of that subgroup.

## How it is actually computed

Almost never a list of 2605 × 2605 matrices. Standard route:

1. Picard–Lefschetz. A loop around a node acts by reflection in the
   vanishing cycle δ:
       T(x) = x ± (x·δ) δ.
   Mon is generated by those reflections once the vanishing cycles
   are known.

2. Beauville-type theorems for the universal family of smooth degree-d
   hypersurfaces: those reflections are Zariski-dense in the full
   orthogonal (or symplectic) group of the vanishing lattice, with a
   short list of low-degree exceptions. That identifies Mon up to finite
   index / Zariski closure. It does not print the matrices.

3. Deligne invariant-cycle: classes fixed by all of Mon come from the
   total space. For the full moduli of smooth sextics that invariant
   subspace is Q h². Extra algebraic classes appear only after shrinking
   B (Noether–Lefschetz locus).

4. Restricted base Mon_Π. If B is only sextics through Π, Mon_Π
   preserves Q h² + Q [Π] and the primitive line Q β. Computing the
   group means generators of π₁ of that NL component, their action on
   (h²)^⊥, then the image and its index. That index is not in this repo.

## What is not a monodromy computation

    h⁴ = 6,  h²·[Π] = 1,  [Π]² = 21,  β² = 750

are the Gram matrix of a few algebraic classes (now theorems in
`Hodge/PlaneSpanPairing.lean`). They say which lines must be invariant
if the family is cut down to “contains Π.” They do not name an element
of Mon or Mon_Π.

The Milnor monodromy of u^5 v + v^6 is an operator on a rank-μ vanishing
lattice of a singularity. It is not the monodromy of the fourfold family.

## What a finished computation would supply

- the lattice L = H^4_prim(X, Z) (or the vanishing lattice of the family),
- a generating set of loops in π₁(B),
- the matrix of each generator on L (or a proof they are Picard–Lefschetz
  reflections in a known root system),
- the subgroup they generate, and usually its index or Zariski closure.

For general sextics that is “Zariski-dense in O(L)” by Beauville +
Picard–Lefschetz + Deligne. For Mon_Π the missing piece is still the
image and the index on the complement of Q h² + Q [Π].
