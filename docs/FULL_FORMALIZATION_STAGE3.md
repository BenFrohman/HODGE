## Scope and exact definitions

This is incremental algebra, not a full geometric Hodge proof. It extends the
reviewed stage-1/2 sources without altering their mathematical statements.
The modulus is used as a degree-24 polynomial; irreducibility is not assumed
or proved. Work is over any field K of characteristic zero with its specified
Q-algebra structure, and any k satisfying the ORIGINAL equation

```
k^24+66081312000*k^18-4811512860000*k^12
  +1167566400000*k^6+583200000 = 0.
```

`Binary K` is the actual `MvPolynomial (Fin 2) K`, not a substitute model of
cohomology. With x=X(0), y=X(1), `binarySextic k = k*x^5*y+y^6`, and
`jacobianIdeal k` is generated by its TWO ACTUAL FORMAL PARTIAL DERIVATIVES.
`Jacobian k` is that polynomial ring's genuine ideal quotient. In particular
we do NOT mistakenly use the unscaled polynomial x^5*y+y^6 for the section.

The code defines H=x^2+bxy+cy^2, G=x^3+dx^2y+exy^2+gy^3 using the existing
`ExplicitCurve.sb/sc/sd/se/sg` evaluated at k. W is the determinant of their
actual formal partial derivatives, not an assumed scalar labelled Jacobian.

- `section_HW_class`: at every root of the original modulus,

```
[H*W] = u0*y^5 + u1*x*y^4 + u2*x^2*y^3 + u3*x^3*y^2
```

  in the actual Jacobian quotient, with constants mapped from K and with

```
u0=c*w0-6; u1=b*w0+c*w1;
```

## Why the common-zero proof is useful

The tenth Python certificate computes the resultant of t^2+bt+c and
 t^3+dt^2+et+g, then supplies a rational Bezout certificate for its value modulo
 the modulus. This stage DOES NOT identify mathlib's Sylvester resultant or
 formally check that tenth numeric Bezout identity. Instead it proves the
 specific geometric input that certificate supplies: H,G have no common
 nonzero homogeneous zero, over EVERY such characteristic-zero field.

Proof: differentiate G^2-H^3=f_k. At a common zero H=G=0 both derivatives of
f_k vanish, so 5k*a^4*b=0 and k*a^5+6b^5=0. The root equation implies k is
nonzero. Thus a=0 or b=0; the second equation forces the other coordinate to
vanish. Every differentiation and implication is checked in Lean. No
irreducibility, fundamental theorem of algebra, numeric resultant computation,
Sylvester API assumption, or external geometric theorem is used here.
