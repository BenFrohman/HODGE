A fourfold is a smooth complex projective variety of dimension 4.
Hodge theory on that dimension is the first place the conjecture is
geometrically open.

## Hodge structure on H^4

    H^4(X, C) = ⊕_{p+q=4} H^{p,q}(X),    conjugate H^{p,q} = H^{q,p}.
    Hdg^2(X) = H^4(X, Q) ∩ H^{2,2}(X).

A surface Z ⊂ X gives [Z] ∈ Hdg^2(X). That is the easy arrow.
The conjecture on a fourfold is the reverse: every such class is ∑ a_i [Z_i].

## Why dimension 4 is the first open case

Lefschetz (1,1): every class in H^2(X, Q) ∩ H^{1,1} is a divisor.
So k = 1 is a theorem on every X.

Hard Lefschetz: L^{n-k} : H^{2k} → H^{2n-2k} is an isomorphism.
Combined with k = 1, complementary codimension is a theorem too.

Dimension ≤ 3 follows: the only nontrivial Hodge classes are divisors
or dual to divisors.

What survives: dim X = 4 and k = 2. Surfaces. Not a divisor, not dual
to a divisor. Clay: known for dimension less than four; unknown in
dimension four.

There is no standalone “Lefschetz (2,2) theorem.” LefschetzTwoTwo in
this repo is a rename of general_fourfold, not a classical theorem.

## Hodge numbers of hypersurface fourfolds in P^5

Middle row of H^4, very general smooth degree-d hypersurface:

| d | h^{4,0} | h^{3,1} | h^{2,2} | b_4 | type |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 3 | 0 | 1 | 21 | 23 | Fano |
| 4 | 0 | 21 | 142 | 184 | Fano |
| 5 | 0 | 120 | 581 | 821 | Fano |
| 6 | 1 | 426 | 1752 | 2606 | Calabi–Yau |

Primitive H^{2,2} is that row minus the ambient class h^2.
Extra rational Hodge classes, when they exist, live in that primitive
lattice. Larger h^{2,2} is not automatically more algebraic classes:
most of H^{2,2} is transcendental until a class is shown to be rational
of type (2,2).

## What is already a theorem on fourfolds

- Linear spaces and quadrics: cohomology generated by linear subspaces /
  Schubert cells. Hodge holds because there is an algebraic basis.
- P^4, Klein quadric Q^4 = Gr(2,4), P^2 × P^2: the three classical islands.
- Cubic fourfolds: Zucker (1977), via the Fano variety of lines.
- Unirational fourfolds: Murre (1977). Uniruled fourfolds: Conte–Murre.
  Quartics and quintics in P^5 are uniruled, so Hodge holds there too
  by that citation, not by a kernel constructor in this repo.
- Very general high-degree hypersurfaces: Noether–Lefschetz. Extra
  rational (2,2) classes vanish; Hdg^2 = Q h^2. Vacuous Hodge.
- Special Fermat hosts: linear cycles generate large pieces of the
  Hodge lattice; Fermat quartic/quintic integrally in the AMV range
  (literature, not twoPlanes).
- Hassett cubics C_d: extra algebraic surfaces on NL divisors. Zucker
  already covers the whole cubic.

Degree ≥ 6 in P^5 is not settled in general. A very general sextic has
only h^2. A special sextic can acquire an extra plane (F ∈ ⟨x3,x4,x5⟩).
A general-but-not-very-general sextic with an extra rational (2,2) class
is the shape of the open problem.

## Periods

The period map records how H^{3,1} and H^{2,2} move. A class becomes
Hodge along an analytic locus. Cattani–Deligne–Kaplan: that locus is
algebraic. Best general evidence that Hodge classes behave like cycles.
It does not produce the cycles.
