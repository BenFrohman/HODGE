# dim R_{12}(F)

Author: Benjamin Stanley Frohman  
License: Apache-2.0

Locked host

$$
F=W(x_0,x_3)+W(x_1,x_4)+W(x_2,x_5),\qquad W=u^5v+v^6.
$$

The projective hypersurface is smooth, so the six partials form a regular sequence of quintics and

$$
R(F)\;\simeq\;R(W)^{\otimes 3},\qquad
H_R(t)=(1+t+t^2+t^3+t^4)^6,\qquad
H_R(1)=5^6=15625.
$$

Hilbert function of one atom \(R(W)\):

| deg | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| dim | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 4 | 3 | 2 | 1 |

Triple convolution at the Griffiths slots:

| piece | dim | Hodge type |
|---|---|---|
| \(R_0\) | 1 | \(H^{4,0}\) |
| \(R_6\) | 426 | \(H^{3,1}\) |
| \(R_{12}\) | **1751** | \(H^{2,2}_{\mathrm{prim}}\) |
| \(R_{18}\) | 426 | \(H^{1,3}\) |
| \(R_{24}\) | 1 | \(H^{0,4}\) |

So

$$
\dim R_{12}(F)=1751=h^{2,2}_{\mathrm{prim}}(V(F)).
$$

Total \(h^{2,2}=1751+1=1752\) after the Lefschetz class \(h^2\). These are the same Hodge *numbers* as a general smooth sextic fourfold. Smooth hypersurfaces of fixed degree in \(\mathbb{P}^5\) have constant Hodge numbers. What can jump on an NL locus is the rank of the *rational* lattice \(H^{2,2}\cap H^4(\mathbb{Q})\), not \(\dim R_{12}\).

This count is not a list of surfaces and not \(\gamma_{\mathrm{bad}}\).

## Lean status (updated 2026-11-10, paper v1.5)

**The equality $\dim_{\mathbb Q} R_{12}(F)=1751$ is now a Lean theorem** for the formalized Jacobian module `jacobianDeg12` of this $F$:

```lean
theorem Hodge.ChainSexticR12.jacobianDeg12_finrank_eq :
    Module.finrank ℚ Hodge.ChainSexticRank.jacobianDeg12 = 1751
```

(`Hodge/ChainSexticR12.lean`; axioms `propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound`). It combines
the existing upper bound `jacobianDeg12_finrank_le` (every degree-12 monomial reduces to the 1751
standard monomials) with the new lower bound `le_finrank_jacobianDeg12` (the 1751 standard
monomials are linearly independent modulo the Jacobian ideal, `stdMonomial_linearIndependent`).
`jacobianDeg12_basis` is an explicit basis indexed by `stdTriples12`. The full step-by-step
argument is in `docs/R12_EQUALITY.md`.

What is still **not** a Lean theorem: the Griffiths identification
$H^{2,2}_{\mathrm{prim}}(X)\cong R_{12}$ (it is a cited theorem, and a named hypothesis
`griffiths` in `ChainSexticHodgeBridge`). So "$h^{2,2}_{\mathrm{prim}}=1751$" is Lean algebra plus
cited Hodge theory, not a Lean theorem about cohomology.

Earlier status (v1.4, superseded): Lean proved only $1 \le \dim R_{12} \le 1751$
(`one_le_finrank_jacobianDeg12`, `jacobianDeg12_finrank_le`).
